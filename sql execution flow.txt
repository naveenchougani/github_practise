SELECT e.department_id, d.department_name, COUNT(e.employee_id) AS total_employees, AVG(e.salary) AS avg_salary
FROM employee e
INNER JOIN department d ON e.department_id = d.department_id
WHERE e.salary > 50000
GROUP BY e.department_id, d.department_name;



Execution Flow:

Explanation of the Query:

FROM and JOIN Clause:

The query starts by selecting data from the employee table (e) and joining it with the department table (d) on the common column department_id.
This INNER JOIN will ensure that only employees who have a matching department_id in the department table will be included in the result.
WHERE Clause:

The WHERE clause filters the rows before grouping them. It restricts the result set to only include employees with a salary greater than 50,000. This means that only employees earning more than 50,000 will be considered in the grouping and aggregation.
GROUP BY Clause:

After filtering the employees, the GROUP BY clause groups the result by department_id (from the employee table) and department_name (from the department table). This means the data will be aggregated for each department.
For each group (department), the query will:
COUNT(e.employee_id): Count the number of employees in that department who have a salary greater than 50,000.
AVG(e.salary): Calculate the average salary for employees in that department who meet the WHERE condition (salary > 50,000).

SELECT e.name, e.salary, d.department_name
FROM employee e
INNER JOIN department d ON e.department_id = d.department_id
WHERE e.salary > (SELECT AVG(salary) FROM employee);


Execution Flow:
Subquery Execution:

The subquery (SELECT AVG(salary) FROM employee) is executed first. It calculates the average salary of all employees and returns a single value, which is used for the comparison in the WHERE clause.
FROM Clause:

The FROM clause identifies employee (e) as the main table.
INNER JOIN:

The INNER JOIN between employee and department is applied, joining employees with their respective departments.
WHERE Clause Filtering:

After the join, the WHERE clause filters out employees whose salary is not greater than the average salary returned by the subquery.
